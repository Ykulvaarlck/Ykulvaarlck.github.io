How it works

Width and height set how big the picture is drawn, not how many pixels it has: they change the text size and leave the resolution alone. Pixels per stud is the resampling control, and Reset puts it back to one output pixel per source pixel at the current width.

Text reads along world -X and stacks up +Z, so the picture hangs in the XZ plane on one Y. Anchors sit at the left edge of the row their text starts on, which keeps both offsets near zero, and they never leave the picture's own rectangle: bottom rows are pulled up, crowded ones look for the nearest free 2cm slot, and every one of them carries the offset that puts its text back where it belongs. If a picture is too small to hold one micro per component they spill out, and the Output panel says how many.

Merging nudges each pixel onto its neighbours' colour where the shift stays under the tolerance, in reading order and wrapping across rows exactly as the colour tag does, which turns scattered near-identical pixels into one long run and one tag. The run re-centres itself on everything it has swallowed rather than sticking with the colour it opened on, and nothing is ever moved further than the tolerance: 1 is around four steps of 8-bit grey and is hard to see, while 2 flattens film grain and faint texture. On a noisy photograph it typically cuts the character count by half or more.

Bricks sit on whole centimetres, so a picture whose size is not a whole number of them cannot be boxed exactly. Its top left corner is pinned to the grid and the leftover sliver, always under one centimetre, is left at the bottom right beside the corner marker.
